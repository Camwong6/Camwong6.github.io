Blue Shield of California

Competitive Intelligence Insights Engine  ·  UC Irvine MSBA Capstone 2026

Overview

Blue Shield of California's Competitive Intelligence team reads earnings calls, SEC filings, press releases, and analyst reports across multiple insurers every week. Their weekly newsletter captured roughly one-eighth of the available transcript content. Synthesizing 720,000+ words of unstructured text manually takes more time than the team had.

We built a three-layer RAG system that lets an analyst run one search query instead of half a day in transcripts. It tracks 9 competitors simultaneously and cuts research time by 10x. Analysts spend that time interpreting findings, not hunting for them.

The Challenge

Blue Shield's CI team spent every week manually reading through eight competitors' SEC filings, earnings transcripts, and news coverage to produce a newsletter. Thousands of pages of financial and regulatory language had to become a two-page brief a senior stakeholder could act on.

Getting executives to trust AI-generated analysis from documents they never read was the central design challenge.

The Users

Primary: The CI team. They knew the source material well but spent too much time locating and synthesizing it.

Secondary: Blue Shield leadership. They consumed the output without touching the underlying data.

The tool had to work at two levels: granular enough for an analyst to verify a specific claim, clean enough for an executive to act on the summary.

Exploratory Data Analysis

We started by running EDA on earnings call transcripts from top competitors, cleaning and preprocessing the data to surface market share trends, demographic patterns, and competitive positioning signals. Those findings shaped what we indexed and how we structured retrieval.

What We Built

The system runs across three layers:

  • Data ingestion: SEC filings, news articles, and real-time web results auto-pull on a 6-hour refresh cycle.
  • RAG core: Hybrid semantic/keyword retrieval (60/40 split) searches 46,000+ indexed chunks. Analysts get relevant results whether they phrase a question precisely or loosely.
  • AI generation: Claude Haiku and Sonnet return structured, source-cited answers grounded in the retrieved corpus. Response caching cuts redundant API calls.

We also built in a four-tier source credibility ranking, domain-specific prompts tuned for health insurance, proactive keyword alerts, and one-click PDF export.

Final Presentation

UX Case Study

I designed four distinct surfaces, each built around a specific job the user needed to do.

1. Signal Dashboard (Monitor)

The overview shows a Competitive Threat Index ranking competitors by signal volume, a topic-level coverage breakdown, and a Signal Momentum Trend line. An analyst checking in each morning can see in seconds whether activity is spiking and where to focus. This screen carries no AI output, only structured signal data. I wanted the team opening it daily as a habit, not only when someone needed to write a brief.

2. Competitor Profiles (Explore)

Each competitor gets a profile card showing revenue, member count, and MLR. Clicking through opens a detail view with a one-paragraph positioning summary, a high/low-priority Threats to Blue Shield section, and a Strengths / Weaknesses / Recent Strategic Moves breakdown. Financials sit at the card level because that's the first thing the CI team reaches for when sizing up a competitor. Strategic interpretation goes one level deeper, in the detail view where readers are already engaged.

3. Ask AI Chat (Query)

The chat interface takes free-form questions and returns sourced answers. The key call was inline citation chips: each [Source 4] tag sits at the specific claim it supports, not appended at the bottom. When citations are buried at the end, analysts skip them and take the answer at face value. Inline, verification is part of reading. The output fed real executive decisions, so the tool needed to hold up to scrutiny.

4. Brief Generator (Synthesize)

I designed the brief generator around the CI team's actual workflow. Every week they built the competitive newsletter from memory and scattered notes. This screen lets an analyst select competitors, choose focus areas (Financial Performance, Market Strategy, Medicare), set a date window, and generate a structured brief. The output appears next to the configuration panel for review without losing the selections. The controls stay explicit so the analyst decides what goes in, not the model.

Key Design Decisions

Inline citation chips. Analysts reading AI output tend to skip citations appended at the bottom and accept the answer. Placing each source tag at the specific claim it supports keeps verification part of reading. The output fed real strategic decisions, so I built the trust mechanisms in from the start.

Four distinct surfaces. The early spec was a single chat interface. I separated monitoring, exploration, querying, and synthesis into their own screens because each task starts from a different cognitive state. An analyst checking morning signals has no use for a query box. A focused search should happen on its own screen.

Revenue and member count at the card level. Watching the CI team evaluate competitors, I saw them reach for financials first as a credibility baseline. Surfacing those metrics at the card level freed the detail view to carry deeper interpretive content without losing the reader's attention.

What I'd Change

I'd make the citation chips interactive. Right now they're static labels. Given more time, I'd make each chip clickable, expanding to show the relevant source excerpt in the chat pane without opening a separate view. That's the moment an analyst decides whether to trust the answer, and the current design leaves it unresolved.

The Broader Pattern

Getting a user to trust and act on AI-generated answers from documents they never read is the core challenge in any AI search product. The citation chip, the sourced brief, the separation of monitoring from querying: these interaction patterns apply beyond health insurance. Products like Ask YouTube are working through the same problem right now.
